Title: Children of Time
Author: Adrian Tchaikovsky
Published: 2015
Pages: 600
Structure: 65 chapters in 8 parts

What happens when civilisations collide, and the remnants of a dying people find a perfect world that is already inhabited? Who deserves the spoils, and how could there possibly be a middle way?

Children of Time is a gripping tale of humanity’s desperate attempt to survive after centuries of conflict render Earth a wasteland. It’s also the grand story of the birth of a new civilisation as it evolves from simplicity to elegant and intelligent complexity.

Steinbeck

East of Eden

by Neil.B.Ennis 18 September 2026 Shortlink | |

Title: East of Eden
Author: John Steinbeck
Written: 1952
Pages: 640 pages
Structure: 55 chapters, split into four parts

Are we condemned to the life that fate allots us through our parents, our upbringing and the circumstances into which we are born, or do we have a choice over who we become?

East of Eden is a vast family saga that explores this question over several generations. It’s a huge book, but I didn’t want it to end. I turned the final page feeling a mixture of hope and despair about human nature, and about our capacity both to damage one another and to choose something better.

Pages: 379

Woolf

Mrs Dalloway

by Neil.B.Ennis 26 June 2026 Shortlink | |

Title: Mrs Dalloway
Author: Virginia Woolf
Written: 1925
Pages: 224
Structure: No chapters.

What does it feel it like to be a middle-aged woman in 1920’s London? Or a traumatised war veteran battling insanity? Or dissatisfied playboy who never really got over being rejected by the love of his life? Mrs Dalloway answers these questions in an intimate way, allowing us to dwell in the minds of different people for one day in June 1922, flitting from mind to mind like a telepathic butterfly.

It’s one of the most unusual novels I have read, and one of the most rewarding.

Eliot

Middlemarch

by Neil.B.Ennis 19 June 2026 Shortlink | |

Title: Middlemarch. A Study of Provincial Life
Author: George Eliot
Written: 1872
Pages: 912
Structure: Prelude, 86 Chapters, Finale, in eight “Books”.

Middlemarch surprised me. It’s brilliantly clever: intimate, and vast at the same time. On the surface it is about life in a small provincial town in the English Midlands around 1830, but under the surface it explores the hopes, motivations and frustrations of people just like us. We can understand what drives each of the characters, even though we may disagree with them. The result feels more than a story, it is almost a detailed philosophy our own minds.
